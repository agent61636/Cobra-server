Jellyfin is the headliner of World 2. It reads every movie and show on the drives, dresses them up with posters and descriptions, and plays them on the TVs, phones and browsers of everyone you let in. When a screen can't play a file as it is, Jellyfin re-encodes it on the fly with the SGIN's Intel GPU.

It lives in the stream pod on cobra-stream (10.6.9.20) and answers on port 8096. Its own files, the database, posters and watch history, sit on the SGIN's internal SSD under /srv/stream/jellyfin. The movies themselves come in read-only from /srv/storage/disks, which the container sees as /media. Jellyfin can look at your library all day long; it can never delete a frame of it.

From outside, it's tv.songofeo.com, and it is the one floor that skips the gate's sign-in. Friends' Roku, Apple TV and phone apps can't show Authelia's login page, so tv. has its own nginx block with no auth_request, and Jellyfin's own login does the guarding.

THE VIDEO POOL

        
wd1 (the WD 6 TB) and wd2 (a folder on the Seagate 24 TB) are joined by mergerfs into one folder, /srv/storage/disks/video. It's only a view: every file sits whole on one drive, and either drive reads fine on its own.

        
New files land on wd1 first (category.create=ff) and spill onto wd2 only once wd1 is down to 100 GB free. That matters, because wd2 lives on the backup drive itself and has no second copy. Keep wd2 for things you could download again.

        
wd1 and wd2 are the only drives that are ever pooled. Music, books and the rest stay one-drive-one-job, so you always know where a file lives.

      

      
THE THREE WAYS TO PLAY

        
Direct Play: the screen plays the file as it is. Zero work for the SGIN. Direct Stream: only the wrapper or audio changes. Cheap. Transcode: the video is decoded and re-encoded live, which is where Quick Sync earns its keep (next page).

QUICK SYNC TURBO MODEHIDDEN POWER

  
The SGIN's Celeron N5095A is a small chip with a big secret: an Intel GPU with Quick Sync, a block of silicon built only for decoding and encoding video. With it switched on, one tiny laptop board can turn a 4K HEVC movie into 1080p H.264 for a phone without breaking a sweat.

THE HUC KEY

        
Jasper Lake can only encode in low-power mode, and low-power mode needs the HuC firmware loaded into the GPU. On Debian 13 that firmware moved into its own package, firmware-intel-graphics, and the driver option enable_guc=2 in /etc/modprobe.d/i915.conf tells i915 to load it.

        
Without it, decoding still works, so everything looks fine, but every transcode fails. The Quick Sync media driver itself, intel-media-va-driver-non-free, lives in Debian's non-free section, which the installer leaves switched off.

      

      
CODES

        SGIN · eels
        sudo dmesg | grep -i -E 'guc|huc'
        Want "HuC ... authenticated". No FAIL or ERROR.
        sudo vainfo --display drm --device /dev/dri/renderD128 | grep -i hevc
        Want HEVC Main10 decode lines and EncSliceLP lines.

PRO TIP: WATCH THE ENGINE

        
While a movie transcodes, sudo intel_gpu_top (as eels) shows the Video row busy. At 0% while htop shows ffmpeg eating the CPU, the transcode is in software. q quits.

In any Jellyfin app, the server is https://tv.songofeo.com with no port. Same address at home, on cellular and on WireGuard; at home the Pi-holes send it straight to the gate inside the house.

      

      
PRO TIP: EPISODE BREAKS

        
Jellyfin apps only ask for intros before movies, never episodes, and the Roku and Apple TV apps ignore web plugins. So cobra-breaks (manual part 6) builds a playlist per show and person, "<Show> — with breaks": episode, filler, episode. It starts at each person's next unwatched episode and rebuilds every day at 4:00.

        
Every break must be a different video (Jellyfin drops repeats from a playlist), so 40 episodes need 39+ filler videos of 3 minutes or less in wd1/filler.

STUTTER BUSTERSMANUAL 3.8C

  

    
"Only some movies stutter" is almost never the file size. A 4 GB two-hour movie averages about 4.5 Mbps, which anything here handles easily. The big files are usually HEVC 10-bit or HDR, often with PGS or ASS subtitles, and when the screen can't play those, Jellyfin transcodes. The stutter comes from that transcode.

    
FIND THE REASON FIRST!

  

  

    
REASON SHOWNFIX

    
Subtitle codec not supportedThe subtitles are being burned in. Turn them off and retest; then pick an SRT track, or set the user's subtitle mode to Smart with image subtitles off by default. The single most common cause.

    
Video codec not supportedUse a client that plays HEVC 10-bit itself: the Android TV / Fire TV app, Jellyfin Media Player, Swiftfin or Infuse. In a browser, Chrome or Edge rather than Firefox.

    
Bitrate exceeds limitClient app → Maximum streaming quality → Auto or highest. Over WireGuard, Dashboard → Playback → Streaming → raise the remote limit.

    
Audio codec not supportedCheap, rarely the stutter. Allow passthrough, or pick the AC3 track.

  

  

    
STILL STUTTERS?

      
Dashboard → Logs → newest FFmpeg.Transcode-…log. The encoder must be h264_qsv or hevc_qsv. libx264 or libx265 means the CPU is doing it: recheck the HuC page.

    

    
SMOOTH BUT JERKY

      
Never buffers, but pans judder? That's a 24 fps film on a 60 Hz TV. Turn on match frame rate (refresh rate switching) in the TV's app.

    

    
WIRED VS WI-FI

      
Retest once with the TV on Ethernet. Smooth wired but not on Wi-Fi means the Wi-Fi is the problem, not Jellyfin. From outside, Xfinity's upload speed is the cap.

    

  

  

    
CODES

      SGIN · eels
      sudo dmesg -T | grep -i -E 'uas|reset|i/o error' | tail -20
      lsusb -t
      Every drive at 5000M. 480M = it fell back to USB 2.
    

    
PRO TIP: FOREVER FIX

      
The best transcode is the one that never happens. A better app on the one TV that keeps transcoding fixes the whole library at once.

JOE SABOTAGE REPORT

      
Pulled from the footlockers, manual parts 3.3 to 3.8c, and the build itself.

    

  

  

    

      
THE MISSING HUC

        
Decode works, the movie list loads, the first transcode dies. Debian 13 moved the GPU firmware out of firmware-misc-nonfree.Install firmware-intel-graphics, keep enable_guc=2, rebuild the initramfs, reboot, and look for EncSliceLP in vainfo.

      

      
THE AV1 TICKBOX

        
Ticking AV1 under hardware decoding looks harmless. Jasper Lake has no AV1 decoder, so AV1 files then fail outright.Leave AV1 unticked; Jellyfin decodes those few files in software.

      

      
THE LOCKED GPU DOOR

        
podsvc not in render, or the container started without keep-groups: the container can't open /dev/dri and everything falls back to the CPU.As eels: sudo usermod -aG render,video podsvc, then log podsvc in again and rerun jellyfin.sh. Its QSV check says pass or fail.

      

      
THE GHOST DRIVE

        
A container sees the drives that were mounted when it started. Replug wd1 and Jellyfin keeps looking at an empty folder, and a scan then marks everything missing.podman pod restart stream first, scan after.

      

    

    

      
EVERYONE IS THE GATE

        
Without Known proxies, every remote viewer looks like 10.6.9.10 on the LAN. Remote bitrate limits never kick in and the logs are useless.Dashboard → Networking → Known proxies: 10.6.9.10. Check: play from the-box, Activity shows the-box's address.

      

      
THE FIRST-VISITOR CROWN

        
Until the setup wizard is finished, whoever opens the page first becomes admin.Finish the wizard on the-box right after the container first starts (admin eels).

      

      
THE "SHOWS" MIX-UP

        
The TV folder on wd1 and wd2 is called series. Point a library at /media/video/shows and it stays empty forever.Shows library = /media/video/series.

      

      
THE POWER-SAVER AMBUSH

        
Laptop guides say "install TLP" or "run powertop --auto-tune". On the SGIN both switch on USB autosuspend, which drops the hub, every drive and the network adapter, its only link.Never install TLP or auto-tune here. Looking with powertop is fine.

      

      
THE :Z RELABEL

        
:Z on /srv/storage/disks would relabel the whole library for one container, and Samba shares those folders too.App data gets :Z; the library never does.
